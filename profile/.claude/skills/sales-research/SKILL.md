# Sales research

Research rules come from `web-research` (primary sources, dates, never fill a cell from memory, no
scraping where forbidden). Contact details: only what the person or company published, with the source;
never guess an address and present it as real.

## Account brief (one page)

```markdown
# <Company>: account brief (<date>)
**In one line:** what they do, for whom, how big (employees, revenue or funding, with sources).
**Why now:** recent triggers worth opening with (funding, hiring, launches, leadership change,
expansion), each dated and linked.
**Fit:** where our product helps them, tied to the triggers; what might block a deal.
**People:** the likely buyer, champion and users: name, title, public profile link; how to reach them
only if published.
**Talking points:** 3 specific openers. **Questions to ask:** 3–5.
**Sources:** links with dates.
```

## Prospect or lead list

Agree on the ideal-customer criteria first (industry, size, region, signals); if the requester gave
none, state the ones you used. One row per company: name, website, industry, size, region, the signal
that qualifies it (with source), contact name and title (published), contact channel (published, with
source, or "not published"), fit score with a one-line reason. Deduplicate against any list they sent.
**Deliver the number asked for.** Directories and "top companies" lists are leads, not
answers: keep researching (more searches, the companies' own sites, local business press, job posts)
until you have that many rows that meet the criteria, or you've tried at least 15 candidates; drop the
ones that fail instead of padding the list. A shorter list is fine only when you say how many you
checked and why the rest failed. Always attach the spreadsheet (`make-spreadsheet`, with a Sources
column) and summarize the top 5 in the thread; don't ask whether they want one.

## Call or meeting prep

The account brief in short, plus: what happened so far (from threads, notes or the CRM if connected),
the goal of this call, the agenda, likely objections with answers, and the next step to ask for.

## Competitor battlecard

Their positioning in their own words (linked), pricing if published (dated), strengths, weaknesses we
can back with sources, how we win, landmines (questions to ask that expose their gaps), and how to answer
"why not them?". No claims you can't source.

## Follow-up after a call

From the notes or transcript: a recap email draft for the rep (thanks, what we heard, agreed next steps
with dates, one clear ask), and the CRM fields to update listed separately.

## Before you send

Every fact dated and linked; nothing promised on price, terms or roadmap; check with `before-you-send`.
