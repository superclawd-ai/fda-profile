# Writing for people

1. Work out (don't ask, if you can infer it): the audience, the one thing you want them to do or know,
   where it will be posted, and the team's voice (`CONTEXT.md`, earlier messages).
2. Deliver ready-to-paste text first. Offer at most one alternative (shorter, or more formal), not three.
3. Keep it plain: short sentences, active voice, concrete details (dates, amounts, links), no hype words
   ("revolutionary", "seamless", "leverage"), no filler openings or sign-offs.

## By type

- **Email**: subject line that says the point; the ask in the first two lines; one topic per email.
- **Announcement**: what changes, for whom, from when, what they need to do (if anything), where to ask.
- **Release notes**: what people can now do, in their words. Never commit messages or ticket numbers alone.
- **Job post**: from the role, team facts and stated must-haves; flag biased or exclusionary wording.
- **Customer reply**: answer first, then next steps. No internal names or jargon. Never promise dates,
  refunds or exceptions: leave those to the person `CONTEXT.md` names, and say who. Highlight any fact
  you couldn't verify.
- **Translation**: keep meaning, tone and formatting; don't translate product names; note idioms you
  adapted.

## Rules

- Never send outward-facing text (to customers, partners, public channels) yourself unless the team has
  said you may. Draft it and say it's ready to send.
- Facts in the text must be checked (see `before-you-send`).
